1. Welcome to SikaFriend

Your illustrated guide to customer service, lending operations and financial administration.

SikaFriend brings borrower records, portfolios, loan approvals, repayments, statements and management reports into one workspace. It also supports workforce administration, payroll, accounting, fixed assets, communications and borrower self-service.

How to use the manual

Use the contents and bookmarks to open a topic. Follow the numbered steps, then compare the result to the screen guide. Button names appear in bold. Available actions depend on your account role and the record's status.

All account names, contact details, balances and employee records shown in the pictures are examples. The screens illustrate how to use the service. Confirm the actual account and reference whenever you perform a live operation.

Your first working session

  1. Open the service address provided by your organization.
  2. Sign in with your personal account.
  3. Confirm your workspace and assigned responsibilities.
  4. Review notifications, outstanding requests and account balances.
  5. Open the relevant customer, loan or operational record.
  6. Read the confirmation message after every action and check the resulting record.
  7. Sign out when finished, especially on a shared device.

Account information and business decisions

The guide explains the service's workflows. Your organization's approved lending, payroll, accounting and customer-service policies determine who may make business decisions and what supporting evidence is required.

2. Accounts, roles and access

Personal accounts

Use the username and password issued to you. Each person should use their own account so approvals and activity history identify the responsible user. Contact an administrator if your account opens the wrong workspace or shows an unexpected assignment.

RoleTypical access
AdministratorAuthorized company administration, borrower and loan operations, governance and financial modules.
ManagementAdministrator workspace with management responsibility for reviews, policy and oversight.
Portfolio officer / agentCurrently assigned active portfolios and their permitted customer and loan records; own agent statement.
BorrowerOwn account, loans, transactions, statements and password.

Portfolio access

An officer's access follows their current active portfolio assignments. When an assignment ends, access to that portfolio ends. Normal customer reassignment updates the related loan ownership while preserving the permanent customer ID and original officer/receipt history.

Requesting and reviewing approvals

The person submitting a request is its requester. The person deciding it is its reviewer. Ordinary staff cannot approve or reject their own requests. Authorized Management and superuser accounts have an exception for ordinary approvals.

Reversals require independent review. A different active administrator must approve or reject a transaction reversal, including requests submitted by Management or a superuser.

Account recovery and changes

Use password recovery where available, or ask an administrator for identity-verified assistance. Give temporary account details privately and change the password promptly. A change of duties may require both an access update and a portfolio reassignment.

When employment ends

Administrators should disable the login, end portfolio assignments and arrange replacement coverage. Retain historical collection, approval and payroll records. Check that the account can no longer be used.

3. Dashboard and navigation

Find your workspace

After sign-in, administrators see the management dashboard, officers open their employee/portfolio context and borrowers open My Account. Use the sidebar on a desktop or the menu button on a phone to move between available modules.

Read the dashboard

Review lending activity, amounts collected, principal exposure, overdue accounts and recent transactions. Open Comprehensive Management Reports for detailed reporting. The Loan Portfolios section below it provides direct access to each portfolio.

Current outstanding balances describe today's recorded book. Collections within a selected period describe money recorded during that window. Pending receipts, held security and customer credit are separate categories; read each label before comparing totals.

Search and filter

  1. Choose the relevant module.
  2. Enter the name, reference or other search value requested by that screen.
  3. Select the date range, status or officer filters needed for your task.
  4. Refresh and read the applied scope.
  5. Open the individual record to verify its details.

Registers may show several pages. An available export can contain all matching rows. In portfolio detail, searching a register does not reduce the complete portfolio's financial summary.

Appearance and mobile use

Use the sun/moon control to choose a light or dark appearance. On a phone, open the menu to reach the same permitted modules. Wide tables may scroll horizontally; review every column before confirming a financial action.

Messages after an action

Read success and validation messages. A request submitted for review is different from an approved transaction. If a result is unclear, find the request or record before submitting again. See Help and common questions.

6. Portfolios and customer IDs

Understand portfolio ownership

A portfolio groups customer and loan records under current officer coverage. Its two-digit number remains permanent; its display name and active officer assignments can be managed by an administrator.

Permanent customer numbers

A customer ID such as SF010001 identifies customer 0001 issued by portfolio 01. The customer keeps that ID after a later ownership change. Issued numbers are not reused. Each issuing portfolio supports customer numbers 0001–1000.

7. Loan applications and review

Before applying

Verify the customer, identity evidence, current portfolio, existing exposure and ability to repay. Complete the organization's required credit checks and lending-policy review.

Submit a loan request

  1. Open New Loan.
  2. Select the borrower and responsible officer.
  3. Enter principal, duration, repayment frequency, installment and dates.
  4. Complete purpose, sector and the required application information.
  5. Record declarations, signatures, guarantor and collateral information where required.
  6. Check the displayed installment count and expected total with the borrower.
  7. Submit once and retain the request reference.

Submission places the application in Loan Requests. The facility is created when an eligible reviewer approves and successfully executes it. Confirm the resulting loan record before promising disbursement.

Reviewer procedure

  1. Open Loan Requests and find the pending application.
  2. Review borrower identity, amount, terms, exposure and supporting information.
  3. Apply the appropriate requester/reviewer rule.
  4. Record a decision note explaining the review.
  5. Select Approve & Execute or Reject.
  6. Confirm the final status and the resulting loan if approved.

Understand the agreed terms

Expected repayment is the displayed installment count multiplied by the installment amount. The agreed ROIC is expected repayment less principal. The service uses fixed conversions for a month in its schedule: four weekly periods or thirty daily periods. Read the displayed count when agreeing terms; these conversions are different from counting calendar dates.

ExampleAmount
PrincipalGHS 1,000.00
10 installments × GHS 120.00Expected total GHS 1,200.00
Agreed ROICGHS 200.00
First settled receiptGHS 120.00: principal GHS 100.00; ROIC GHS 20.00.
Balance after that receiptGHS 1,080.00.

Loan status

Active means a balance remains within the applicable default threshold. Closed means recorded repayments and approved security applications cover the agreed repayment. Default means a balance remains and overdue periods exceed the configured grace threshold.

Several partial receipts do not equal several paid installments. Compare the money applied to scheduled amounts. Refer disputed agreement details to management and retain the supporting agreement.

Review an existing loan

Open the loan to inspect borrower context, terms, payments, security and statements. A successful reversal can reopen a closed loan if a balance becomes due again.

8. Security deposits

Security funds belong to the customer and are tracked separately from repayments and customer credit.

Review required and held security

Open the loan's Security Deposit action. Compare the required amount to funds currently held. Collections and incoming transfers add to held funds; refunds, approved applications and outgoing transfers reduce them.

Collect security

  1. Verify the customer, approved loan and displayed requirement.
  2. Choose Collection and enter the received amount, method, date and reference.
  3. Complete the required customer verification or consent details.
  4. Submit for approval.
  5. A reviewer matches the request to receipt evidence and approves the valid action.
  6. Check the updated held balance and secured status before proceeding with the loan.

Refund, apply or carry forward

ActionWhen to use it
RefundReturn available held security on an eligible closed loan, with actual refund evidence.
Default applicationApply available security to principal on an eligible Default loan. The amount is limited by both held funds and principal due.
Carry-forwardMove available security from a closed loan to a receiving loan of the same borrower, within its remaining requirement.

Example

A GHS 1,000 loan with GHS 100 required security holds GHS 100 after collection. This does not reduce agreed loan repayment. If an eligible GHS 50 security application is approved later, held security falls to GHS 50 and outstanding principal falls by GHS 50.

Status and corrections

Required indicates a shortfall; Secured indicates the requirement is met. Refunded or Applied indicates funds have been used through those actions. Inspect the full history when several types of movement exist.

Use an approved reversal for an erroneous deposit record. Funds already used by a later refund, application or transfer may need that later movement resolved first. Removing an erroneous security collection does not undo an already valid loan disbursement.

9. Recording and verifying repayments

Record a payment

  1. Open the correct loan and confirm its customer and outstanding balance.
  2. Select Add Payment or Record Payment.
  3. Enter the actual received amount and receipt date.
  4. Select the real payment method and enter a traceable cash, bank or mobile-money reference.
  5. Compare the entry to the payment evidence.
  6. Select Save Payment once.
  7. Check whether it is settled or pending, then inspect the loan balance and history.

Amount and allocation

A new repayment must be positive and cannot exceed the remaining agreed balance. The service allocates the applied amount between principal and ROIC according to the loan terms and remaining components. Pending and reversed receipts do not reduce the current settled balance.

Confirm a pending receipt

  1. Open Pending Payments.
  2. Match customer, loan, amount, method, date and reference to independent evidence.
  3. Confirm only when funds have arrived and the entry is correct.
  4. Reject an invalid entry through the displayed action and retain the review explanation.
  5. Verify one settled receipt and the correct balance change.

Partial payments

For an installment of GHS 120, three receipts of GHS 20 total GHS 60. They cover half an installment. Follow up on the amount still due rather than counting receipt rows.

Correct a settled mistake

Use Transaction Reversals when a verified settled receipt was recorded incorrectly. After approval, record any legitimate replacement separately and cross-reference the correction. Keep the original receipt evidence.

Recording a repayment or reversing its entry does not itself transfer money between a bank, provider or customer. Verify the actual funds separately.

10. Payment requests and reconciliation

Request versus receipt

A payment request tracks the customer's submission and reference. A verified receipt records the payment applied to the loan. A Pending request is not proof that money has arrived.

Review a collection

  1. Open Payment Reconciliation.
  2. Locate the client/provider reference and correct customer/loan.
  3. Compare amount and date with actual bank, cash or provider evidence.
  4. Use the displayed reconciliation action only when the evidence supports it.
  5. Record failure or refer an exception when evidence does not agree.
  6. Check the final status, linked receipt and balance effect.
StatusMeaning
PendingSubmission recorded; verification outstanding.

SucceededSuccess evidence recorded; check reconciliation.
ReconciledThe linked repayment has been posted.
FailedThe recorded collection outcome is unsuccessful.
Manual ReviewAn exception needs authorized staff attention.

Duplicate references and corrections

Check whether a receipt already exists before reconciling again. An original provider receipt remains identifiable after reversal. A genuine later payment needs its own evidence and reference. Escalate a disputed or duplicate reference to the responsible administrator.

Borrower submissions

Submitting a portal request does not automatically charge the customer's bank or mobile-money account. The borrower should follow the company's payment instructions and retain their actual receipt until the account shows verification.

11. Customer credit and balances

Credit held for the customer

Where an existing account includes money received above its agreed repayment, that excess is held as customer credit. It is separate from income, applied repayment and security deposits.

Worked example

A loan has GHS 1,000 principal and GHS 1,200 agreed total. Verified receipts total GHS 1,260. GHS 1,200 settles the loan; GHS 60 remains customer credit. The customer's original cash receipt history still totals GHS 1,260.

Amount shownInterpretation
Cash receivedAll active verified receipt money.
Applied repaymentCash received less held customer credit.
Principal / ROIC paidComponents of repayment applied to the agreement.
Customer creditExcess held for the customer.
Security heldSeparate customer security funds.
Loan balanceAgreed repayment less applied receipts and approved security applications, with a minimum of zero.

Refund or later use

Refer a request to refund or apply credit to management and finance. They must approve the actual movement and its record. Displayed credit does not automatically create a payout or transfer to another loan. Keep customer-credit decisions separate from security-deposit carry-forward.

After a reversal

An approved reversal removes the erroneous receipt's repayment and credit effect from current totals. The statement keeps the original amount and a separate reversal movement. Check the revised loan balance and credit before issuing a new statement.

Print or download

  1. Open the loan, borrower or agent profile and select Statement.
  2. Verify the person, reference, company identity and generation date.
  3. Review terms, receipt history, balance, security and credit.
  4. Select Print Statement for the print dialog. Choose A4 and inspect the preview.
  5. Select Open PDF for the prepared PDF or Download PDF to save a copy.
  6. Share only with an authorized recipient through the approved channel.

Read pending and reversed entries

Pending or void entries remain visible for clarity and do not reduce the current settled balance. Approved reversals retain the original transaction and add a negative movement dated on approval, with the reference, approver and reason.

Security collections and transfers affect held funds. Approved security applications reduce principal. Customer credit is shown separately. If a statement flags agreement details for review, refer them to management before using the disputed terms for a new decision.

Statement access and scope

Borrowers see their own records. Officers see currently permitted loan portfolios and their own agent statement. The agent statement's collection coverage follows its visible current portfolio book; it is not a lifetime total of all collections in former assignments. Employee payroll history stays tied to that employee.

Keep copies private

A downloaded PDF contains account information. Check email/phone recipients carefully and store printed copies securely. Ask the customer to quote the statement reference when reporting a concern.

13. Approved transaction reversals

Correct a verified recorded mistake with independent administrator review and a clear permanent record.

When a reversal is appropriate

Examples include a duplicate settled receipt, receipt entered against the wrong loan, incorrect cashbook entry, eligible unsettled invoice/bill, paid payroll recorded in error, eligible manual journal or depreciation correction.

A genuine cash refund, salary recovery or provider refund is a separate money movement. Arrange and document it through the approved business process.

Submit the request

  1. Open Transaction Reversals, or select Request reversal on the original record.
  2. Choose the correct transaction and compare amount, date, reference and customer/accounts.
  3. Read the proposed effect and supporting evidence.
  4. Enter a clear reason of 10–4,000 characters. Include the error and intended correction.
  5. Add an evidence reference where available, up to 180 characters.
  6. Complete the acknowledgement and submit once.
  7. Retain the pending case reference. Submission alone does not change balances.

Example reason: “Receipt entered twice from reconciliation reference SAMPLE-017. Retain the first verified receipt and remove this duplicate entry.”

Independent reviewer procedure

  1. A different active administrator opens the case from Approvals or the reversal register.
  2. Verify the original transaction, reason and cash evidence.
  3. Review the proposed ledger offsets and any later use of the funds.
  4. Enter a meaningful review note of at least ten characters.
  5. Select Approve & execute reversal or Reject request.
  6. Check the final result and affected account.
  7. Print the case if needed for the reconciliation file.

Management and superuser requesters also require a different administrator. The independence rule applies to both approval and rejection.

Effects by transaction

TransactionApproved correction
Settled loan receiptRemoves the receipt from current collections/credit and restores principal/ROIC due; original evidence remains.
Security collectionReduces held funds that are still available.
Security refundRestores the recorded refunded amount to held funds.
Security applicationRestores held security and increases outstanding principal.
Security carry-forwardRestores the original deposit and reduces the receiving deposit.
Posted cashbook entryOffsets the posting, including both accounts of a bank transfer.
Eligible unsettled invoice/billVoids the document, offsets its accrual and removes its outstanding aging.
Paid payroll runCancels the run and offsets recorded postings while keeping employee lines.
Eligible manual journalPosts an equal opposing entry.
Eligible depreciation runCancels the run and restores affected asset book values through offsets.

Eligibility and dependent transactions

Pending or already reversed receipts are not eligible settled sources. A collection or transfer cannot remove security already used by later movements. Depreciation may require later dependent runs to be corrected first. Settled/partly settled documents, loan disbursements and asset disposals need finance/management reconciliation.

Result and evidence

Completed cases receive a permanent reversal reference. Original amounts, dates, allocations and journals remain available. Ledger offsets use the approval date in the current open accounting period. Statements explain the original and its separate negative movement.

Failed, rejected or withdrawn cases

A failed execution does not apply a partial financial correction. Read the case result and refer the cause for review. If the original transaction changed, a new request may be necessary. Rejection does not change balances. A requester may withdraw a pending case using the displayed action.

Verify before replacing

Confirm the case is Executed, the original is marked appropriately, balances changed once, offsets agree and the statement explains the correction. Record a justified replacement separately with its own accurate receipt evidence.

Evidence files

18. Accounting and financial controls

Set up the finance workspace

Review your loan

Select your loan to read principal, agreed repayment, schedule, payments, balance and security information. Check the reference before making a payment. Your account is limited to your own records.

Submit a payment request

  1. Choose the correct loan and payment action.
  2. Enter the requested amount and displayed details.
  3. Follow the company's actual cash, bank or mobile-money instructions.
  4. Keep your receipt or transfer confirmation and reference.
  5. Wait for verification and check the resulting repayment.

Submitting a request does not automatically charge your bank or mobile-money account. Pending means verification remains outstanding.

Pending or reversed history

A pending item does not reduce your final settled balance. A reversed receipt remains visible with correction information. If this differs from your payment evidence, contact the company with loan reference, amount, date and receipt reference.

Print a statement

Use Print My Account Statement or the loan statement action. Verify your identity and loan details, then choose Print, Open PDF or Download PDF. Keep the copy private.

Change password and get assistance

Use Change Password and choose a strong unique password. Never send it to an officer or put it in a payment reference. Sign out on a shared device. Tell the company if your contact details change or you suspect account misuse.

23. Bulk imports and exports

Prepare an import

Borrowers, loans, workforce and assets have import screens for authorized users. Download the current sample for that module and format. Follow its required columns and related-reference guidance.

  1. Prepare an approved, checked file from the sample.
  2. Keep IDs as text so leading zeros remain intact.
  3. Check dates, money values and required portfolio/officer/category/account references.
  4. Choose Create only for new records or Upsert only for intended updates.
  5. Arrange a verified backup and a small representative trial with the responsible administrator.
  6. Read created, updated and error counts.
  7. Check row-level errors and resulting records before retrying.
  8. Retain the approved file and results privately.

A file can contain successful rows and error rows. Check the outcome before repeating the whole batch. Imports do not automatically follow the interactive loan-application review steps; apply the organization's approval process before importing financial records.

Export for review

Choose the required report/register, verify filters and dates, then use an available export. Confirm whether it contains all matching rows. Customer and payroll exports require authorized storage and recipients.

24. Daily and period-end routines

Start of day

  1. Review dashboard position and officer coverage.
  2. Check pending loan requests, approvals and reversal cases.
  3. Review pending receipts and collection exceptions.
  4. Prioritize overdue accounts using aging and customer context.
  5. Inspect notifications and enquiries needing follow-up.

Officer field cycle

Confirm assigned customers, review amounts due, verify any collected funds and record accurate references. Report disputes promptly. Reconcile actual cash to the day's entered receipts before handing over.

End of day

  1. Reconcile cash, bank and mobile-money evidence to verified receipts.
  2. Explain applied repayment, credit and security differences.
  3. Assign unresolved confirmations and exceptions.
  4. Review approved changes and their balance effects.
  5. Retain control totals/evidence and confirm the organization's backup arrangements.
  6. Sign out and secure records/devices.

Weekly and month-end

Review principal exposure, aging, officer performance, credit/security liabilities and outstanding cases each week. At month-end, verify payroll, reconcile ledger and cashbooks, review aging, post approved depreciation, prepare management outputs and close the period only after review.

Record preparer, reviewer, cutoff and unresolved exceptions. Use the checklists in Appendix A.

25. Privacy and safe everyday use

Account security

Use a personal account and unique password. Lock your device when unattended and sign out after shared-device use. Report unexpected access, unfamiliar activity or incorrect role/portfolio coverage promptly.

Handle records carefully

Verify recipients before sharing statements or payroll exports. Keep identity images, financial evidence and printed records in approved storage. Avoid placing private documents in public website content or personal messaging accounts.

Support information

When asking for help, provide the task, time, record reference and visible message. Share only the account detail needed to investigate. Never send your password.

Interrupted sessions

If connection or power fails during a submission, sign in again and inspect the request/record before repeating it. Check any actual funds received independently. Refer an unclear result for review.

Appendix A. Practical checklists

Before saving a receipt

  • Correct borrower and loan.
  • Verified actual funds and method.
  • Accurate date, amount and reference.
  • Amount within the remaining agreed balance.
  • One saved receipt and the expected status/balance.

Before approving a reversal

  • Different active administrator.
  • Verified original transaction and supporting evidence.
  • Clear reason and meaningful review note.
  • Dependent movements and current period reviewed.
  • Completed result, revised balance and offset entries checked.
  • Case reference and statement retained.

Before distributing a report

  • Correct period, filters and definitions.
  • Cash, principal, credit and security reconciled.
  • Ledger and operational differences explained.
  • Reviewer and authorized recipient confirmed.
